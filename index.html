        How to read this page
        • Status labels use well-known thresholds where they exist: the Fed's 2% inflation goal, the Sahm rule's 0.5 trigger, a yield curve below zero, VIX above 20 or 30, jobless claims above 250K.
        • The Fed's 2% goal is set on core PCE prices, so only that tile is graded against "the goal". CPI, the inflation number in the news, usually runs a little higher than PCE, but not always.
        • Everything else is judged against its own last 10 years (1 year for gold and bitcoin): "higher than 90% of readings" means it's been higher than now only 10% of the time.
        • "Sharp rise / drop" means the latest monthly move is more than twice its usual size for that number, in the direction that's bad news. A 3% move is routine for oil but huge for the dollar. A big move the good way (oil falling, say) shows as "Falling fast" / "Rising fast" with no warning.
        • Inflation momentum shows the last 3 months at a yearly pace, because the year-over-year figure is slow to show turns.
        • Monthly government data arrives weeks after the month ends and can be revised. Each tile shows when it's from and when the next release is due.
        • Recession signals are warning lights, not forecasts. The Sahm rule fired falsely in 2024, and the yield curve says nothing about timing.
        • Data refreshes every hour. If a source fails, the last good value stays up and is marked Delayed.

        Data: FRED, St. Louis Fed, CoinGecko, Polymarket, Wikimedia, and BBC, Al Jazeera, The Guardian and NPR news feeds. For information only, not financial advice.
